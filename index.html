The problem

Setting a take-profit or stop-loss meant leaving the chart, reading a number off it, and typing that number into a form — while the price kept moving. The chart is where the decision actually happens; the form was in the way.

What I built
  • Interactive TP/SL lines drawn onto the TradingView chart, draggable in place, with order state staying in sync in both directions.
  • Live reconciliation between the chart, the order form and the incoming WebSocket price stream, so the numbers never disagree mid-drag.
  • Designed and shipped independently — from the interaction idea through to production.
Outcome

The first implementation of its kind among exchanges in Iran, and now one of the features users single out when they talk about the platform.

The problem

Traders keep the platform open all day. With continuous WebSocket streams feeding prices, order-book depth and charts, memory climbed steadily through a session and took stability and responsiveness down with it.

What I did
  • Profiled the trading pages and traced the growth back to the JavaScript logic handling high-frequency updates.
  • Reworked that logic — how streamed data is stored, reused and released — rather than patching symptoms.
  • Used AI assistants as part of the profiling and optimization loop across many pages of the platform, with every change verified against a real measurement.
Outcome

Browser memory on the affected pages fell from roughly 350MB to 162MB — a 54% reduction — with a matching improvement in stability over long sessions.
